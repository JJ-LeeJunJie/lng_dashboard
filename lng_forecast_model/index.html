Reading the model

Analyst notes

Scale

Northeast Asia runs 2.7× South Asia's average monthly demand and 2.7× Southeast Asia's — China alone accounts for the bulk of it, so any China-specific assumption dominates every regional aggregate.

Contract book

The aggregate contract book declines through the 2030s even as total LNG supply keeps growing (see the contracted-vs-spot chart) — spot exposure (the orange band) is a thin sliver in the near-term forecast and becomes the visibly larger share of supply by 2040. Whether new long-term contracts get signed to backfill that (not modeled here) is the single biggest swing factor in this report.

Origin concentration

"Portfolio / Unspecified" volume — contracts with no traceable single origin plant — is the largest exporting-origin bucket in the model, ahead of the Middle East and Australia individually. That's a real feature of how the global LNG trade is structured today, not a gap in this pull.

Open question

Approach B's aggregate supply overtakes demand from ~2033 — traced to China's production-CAGR (+5.5%/yr) running ahead of its own demand-CAGR (+2.2%/yr). Not yet dampened; worth a targeted per-country supply-vs-demand pass before treating the aggregate surplus as real.

Growth ranking

Vietnam (+13.1%/yr), Philippines (+10.3%/yr) and Bangladesh (+8.4%/yr) post the steepest LNG-import CAGRs to 2040 — all three off a small base, all three with essentially no offsetting domestic production of their own.
